A gravity-powered cart carries no engine, no battery and no motor. It converts elevation into speed on a descent, and everything else — the fork geometry, the brakes, the frame — exists to control that speed safely. The category lives or dies on one question: what happens the first time the rider needs to stop hard, on gravel or on ice, at the bottom of a run? GravityCart's answer starts with the chassis, not the marketing copy.

## The chassis is the product

The GravityCart JM-001 is the complete platform: a CE-certified chassis with a forged fork, priced at €3,499 and shipped with the wheel assembly already mounted. CE certification means the chassis has been assessed against the same category of safety requirements that govern equipment sold into the EU market — it is a compliance mark, not a slogan. The forged fork is the same manufacturing choice used in automotive and motorcycle steering components, because forging produces a denser, more fatigue-resistant part than casting, at the exact point where a gravity cart takes its steering loads.

## Braking is where a gravity vehicle earns trust

A cart with no motor still needs to shed speed reliably, repeatedly, and without fade. The Wheel Assembly — Summer Kit (€899) is a forged-aluminum wheel assembly built around dual-piston brake calipers for warm-weather descents. Dual-piston calipers clamp the rotor from two points instead of one, distributing clamping force more evenly and giving the rider more consistent, more controllable braking feel run after run — the same reason dual-piston and multi-piston calipers are standard on performance vehicles rather than economy ones.

## One chassis, two seasons

The engineering decision that makes GravityCart an all-season platform is the mount point itself: one interface, two assemblies. The Wheel Assembly — Summer Kit and the Ski Assembly — Winter Kit (€799, CNC-milled for snow and ice descents) bolt to the identical chassis mount point on the JM-001, and the swap takes under five minutes. There is no second chassis to buy, no separate certification to trust, and no compromise between the assembly built for gravel and the assembly built for ice — because both are built to bolt to the same automotive-grade frame.

## Keeping it that way

Braking hardware and chassis tolerances only stay safe if they are maintained to spec. The Precision Maintenance Kit (€129) supplies brake fluid, sealed bearing grease and torque-spec hardware for seasonal conversion and routine upkeep — the same categories of consumable an automotive service interval covers, sized for a gravity cart's actual duty cycle.
